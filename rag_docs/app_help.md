# App Help Guide

This guide explains how to use the solar-planning app. It is written for Pakistani homeowners, not engineers.

## Create your home

**What it does:** The app creates a profile for your home so its estimates can reflect your city, electricity distributor, connection type, and household needs.

**How to use it:**
1. Open the Home section and select **Create home**.
2. Enter your city or area and select your electricity distributor, such as LESCO, K-Electric, IESCO, or another DISCO.
3. Add basic home details, including number of rooms, people, and major appliances.
4. Save the home profile.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not inspect your roof, wiring, meter, earthing, or electrical safety. It cannot confirm whether your DISCO will approve net metering or whether your connection meets its requirements.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not select the wrong DISCO or connection type. Net-metering rules and technical requirements can differ by distributor.

## Appliance inventory

**What it does:** The appliance inventory is your list of electrical items, such as fans, lights, refrigerator, deep freezer, AC, water pump, washing machine, iron, and TV. The app uses it to estimate household load.

**How to use it:**
1. Go to **Appliances**.
2. Add each appliance and its quantity.
3. Enter the wattage from the appliance label if known; otherwise use the app's typical value.
4. Mark which appliances must run during load-shedding.
5. Save the list.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not measure the real electricity used by your appliances. Actual use changes with appliance age, settings, outdoor temperature, and family habits.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not forget water pumps, deep freezers, irons, and ACs. These can have high running power or a large starting surge.

## Photo detection

**What it does:** You can upload a photo of an appliance label or inverter display, and the app will try to identify useful information such as appliance wattage or an error code.

**How to use it:**
1. Open the photo-upload option.
2. Take a clear, well-lit photo of the appliance label or inverter screen.
3. Make sure the model number, wattage, or error code is readable.
4. Review the detected result before saving it.
5. Correct any wrong detail manually.

**What it does NOT do:** Photo detection is not always correct. It may misread blurry photos, unusual labels, damaged screens, or unfamiliar inverter models. It does not replace the appliance label or the inverter manual.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not rely on a detected value without checking it. If the app reads the wrong model or wattage, the sizing estimate can become misleading.

## Sizing results

**What it does:** The app turns your home and appliance information into a planning estimate for solar panels, inverter capacity, and battery backup.

**How to use it:**
1. Complete your home profile and appliance inventory.
2. Open **Sizing** or **Results**.
3. Review the estimated system size, essential backup loads, and battery requirement.
4. Compare the result with your actual electricity bills and load-shedding needs.
5. Share the result with an installer for a site-specific design.

**What it does NOT do:** Sizing is a planning estimate, not an installer quote. It cannot see roof shading, panel direction, wiring condition, local grid constraints, voltage fluctuation, or the exact equipment an installer will supply.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not treat the suggested system size as final. A rooftop survey and professional design are still necessary.

## Bills and calibration

**What it does:** You can add electricity-bill information so the app can compare its estimates with your real household consumption. Calibration helps adjust assumptions when actual usage differs from the initial estimate.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Bills**.
2. Add recent electricity bills, including monthly units and billing period where available.
3. Save the details.
4. Return to sizing results and review whether the estimate needs adjustment.
5. Update bills periodically as your usage changes.

**What it does NOT do:** It cannot access your utility account automatically or guarantee that estimated savings will match your future bill. Tariffs, taxes, fuel adjustments, and usage can change.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not enter only one unusually high or low bill. Use several recent bills for a more realistic picture.

## Weather forecast

**What it does:** The weather forecast gives an indication of expected sunlight conditions and how they may affect solar production.

**How to use it:**
1. Open the **Weather** section.
2. Select your home or location.
3. Review the forecast for the coming days.
4. Use it to understand why production may be higher on clear days and lower during cloud, rain, or smog.
5. Check actual production in your inverter app as well.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not measure your panels directly. Weather forecasts can be wrong, and local shading, dust, panel angle, and equipment condition also affect output.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not assume a sunny forecast means your system will produce its maximum rated power. Real output is usually lower than panel nameplate capacity.

## Check-ins

**What it does:** Check-ins let you record how your system is performing, such as daily production, battery status, outages, or unusual inverter behaviour.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Check-ins**.
2. Add a new entry when you notice something important.
3. Record the date, solar production, battery condition, and any inverter error code.
4. Add a photo of the inverter screen if relevant.
5. Review previous entries before contacting an installer.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not diagnose the fault or repair the system. It is a record-keeping tool that helps you and your installer understand patterns.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not wait until the system fails completely. Record repeated warnings, low production, or unusual behaviour early.

## Recommendations

**What it does:** The app provides general suggestions based on the information you entered, such as possible system-size ranges, battery considerations, or maintenance reminders.

**How to use it:**
1. Complete your home profile, appliance list, and bills.
2. Open **Recommendations**.
3. Read each suggestion and check whether it matches your needs.
4. Use the suggestions to prepare questions for an installer.
5. Do not purchase equipment based only on an in-app recommendation.

**What it does NOT do:** Recommendations are not a professional engineering design, financial guarantee, or installation quote. They cannot account for every site condition or local regulation.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not buy a larger battery or inverter only because the app suggests it. Confirm the recommendation with a qualified installer.

## What-if comparison

**What it does:** The what-if comparison lets you see how changing assumptions may affect the estimate. For example, you can compare a larger battery, more panels, or a different backup-load choice.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **What-if** or **Compare**.
2. Select the option you want to change, such as battery capacity or appliance use.
3. Save the scenario.
4. Compare the scenarios side by side.
5. Keep the scenario that best matches your real needs and budget.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not calculate an exact installation price, financial return, or guaranteed performance. It is a comparison tool for planning, not a substitute for installer quotations.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not compare scenarios with unrealistic assumptions, such as running every appliance during every outage. Use realistic essential-load scenarios.

## Model transparency

**What it does:** The model transparency page explains the assumptions and limits behind the app's estimates. It helps you understand why results may change when you update appliances, bills, or location.

**How to use it:**
1. Open **Model transparency** or **How estimates work**.
2. Read the assumptions used for solar production, appliance consumption, and battery backup.
3. Check which inputs most affect your result.
4. Update incorrect information in your home profile or appliance list.
5. Use the page to understand the difference between an estimate and a professional design.

**What it does NOT do:** It does not make the app's estimate exact or replace an installer's site survey. The app works with the information you provide and general assumptions.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Do not assume that a detailed-looking result is automatically accurate. Better inputs improve the estimate, but they do not remove the need for professional verification.

## When to talk to a human installer

Contact a qualified solar installer or electrician promptly if any of the following apply:

- You notice a burning smell, smoke, melted plastic, sparking, or hot cables.
- A battery is swollen, leaking, unusually hot, or smells chemical.
- A breaker trips repeatedly or the inverter shuts down repeatedly.
- You see damaged, loose, corroded, or discoloured wiring.
- The inverter shows a fault that returns after a restart.
- Solar production remains very low during clear weather.
- You plan to connect to the grid or apply for net metering.
- You are considering changes to panels, batteries, wiring, earthing, or protection equipment.
- You experience frequent voltage fluctuation, flickering lights, or appliance damage.
- You are unsure whether an appliance, inverter, or battery is safe to use.

Do not open the inverter, battery enclosure, distribution board, or DC wiring yourself. Keep children and pets away from electrical equipment, and photograph the inverter model and error code before calling for support.
